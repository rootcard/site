What {{home.name}} is

One home among many.

A rootcard identity lives at a home: a server that keeps its public log and encrypted vault and lets you sign in. {{home.name}} is a public home for the {{home.realm}} realm, run on the open-source rootcard-host. It is not the rootcard project's central server, because there is none. Who runs it and what it keeps.

What it keeps

Your public log, your public profile and your vault, encrypted in your browser before it is sent. You can download all of it as one file at any time.

What it cannot do

Read your vault, learn your password, reset it for you, or sign in as you. Your id is signed by your own recovery key, so this server cannot change or forge it.

Where else you can go

Any other rootcard home, or your own server with rootcard-host. With the recovery card you move in minutes, and your id, handle and password come along.

Questions

Good to know

What happens if I lose the recovery card?

The identity keeps working with your password, but it can no longer be reset or moved. If you then also forget the password, the identity is gone. Nobody, {{home.name}} included, can recover it: that is the point.

What if {{home.name}} disappears?

With the recovery card your id and handle survive on any other host. With the card and a downloaded account file, everything moves: private data, profile and passwords.

Why is the key tool a file I download?

The recovery key is never typed into a page a server delivers live, because that server could change the page. The key tool is a single HTML file from the npm package that you open from your own disk. It talks only to the hosts you enter.

Can services link my accounts?

Every service gets its own proof, valid only for that service. If you do not want services to link your accounts, create several identities: they cannot be linked by their public data.
